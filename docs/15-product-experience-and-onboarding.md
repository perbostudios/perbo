# Product Experience and Onboarding

## First run

From the repository, run `./scripts/setup-local.mjs` (`node scripts/setup-local.mjs` on Windows). It checks Node 22+ and Git, fetches the pinned pnpm, installs dependencies, builds the CLI and the desktop, and opens Perbo; `--no-launch` builds without opening a window.

Inside Perbo:

1. Enter a name. It is stored on this machine and stamped on the tickets you admit — no account is created and nothing is sent anywhere.
2. Sign in to Claude Code and/or Codex through their own CLIs (`claude auth login`, `codex login`). Perbo runs on whichever subscription is already signed in and needs no API key ([D-093](11-open-decisions.md)). Choose executor and reviewer models, and each one's effort, from what each CLI reports.
3. Choose a repository already checked out on this machine — nothing is cloned. Run its environment check and save the proposed configuration; setup will not finish until that completes.

Re-running the setup script preserves your name, repositories and provider logins. The CLI it builds keeps tickets, contracts and evidence under `.perbo/` in whichever repository you point it at.

## The desktop

### Home and creating a task

Home lists the tickets the loop is carrying, and the right corner of its header counts them by colour, left to right: how many wait on your answer, how many stopped and how many wait on your merge decision, then how many completed, each only where there are any; the greeting beneath counts the tickets running and repeats how many wait on your answer, on your merge decision and completed. Completed names a ticket whose merge is decided — merged, or closed without merge — and nothing else, on Home and on the rail; a pull request still waiting on that decision is named for what it waits on. Work before the loop — a planning, a spec, a plan not yet approved — is in **Create**'s picker, because approving is what starts the loop ([D-129](11-open-decisions.md)). With no tickets on it, the foot of Home says "Nothing admitted yet" at its left with a primary **Create a task** button at its right, the two on the page itself with no bar around them. Search (⌘K) matches title, key, branch or repository; filters narrow to what needs you (yellow), what's running (no colour), what's stopped (red), what waits on your merge decision (green) or what's completed; sort is recently opened, newest, oldest, title or furthest along; recently opened, which Home opens on, lists the pull requests waiting on the merge decision (green) first, then what needs you (yellow), then what stopped (red), then what's running, and last, under every colour, each ticket whose merge is decided — merged, or closed without merge — each group most recently opened first, with a ticket never opened after those, the most recently admitted first, and newest and oldest keep those five groups in that order and sort each by when its ticket last moved; title and furthest along keep each ticket whose merge is decided below all the rest. A ticket is archived by hand and never on its own: a stopped one, and a completed one; one whose pull request still waits on the merge decision stays on Home ([D-097](11-open-decisions.md)). A ticket whose merge is decided carries a check mark in its progress wheel where the others show how far along they are, and Archive on its card as a stopped one does. Archive all files every ticket on Home that can be archived, and says how many. Each card carries its stage, branch, repository and diff size (`+added −removed · files`, or "no diff yet" before a first run) — its ticket key shows as `#142`, with the `PRB-` prefix dropped — and under them the contract's outcome sentence, the line under the title on its contract page, whole, wrapping onto more lines where the card is too narrow for one ([D-NEW-nothing-shown-is-cut](11-open-decisions.md)), with the line kept blank while it is read, or where the contract cannot be; a red card carries its own line instead — that its run stopped and its work and evidence are kept, or, for a ticket cancelled or rolled back, its criteria count and that it stays until archived — and its stage reads "loop stopped". A card's colour says where its ticket stands: yellow where a finding waits on your answer mid-loop, red where the loop stopped and will not reach the end on its own, green where the journey ended — a pull request opened, merged or closed without merge, or a local run finished with no pull request to merge — and none anywhere else in the loop; the records being read again never changes it. The Home icon in the rail counts only what is still owed a look: a ticket waiting on your answer, stopped, or with its pull request waiting on the merge decision, until you open it after it came to stand there — read from the ticket's own history and when its page was last opened, a page open as it comes to stand there counting as that opening — and again if it comes to need you once more; a ticket whose merge is decided never counts. Each such card carries a small blue circle in its top-right corner until you open it, and a screen reader hears the circle's words with the card. The icon shows those counts one colour at a time, in the header's order, each in its colour and each held ten seconds, shrinking away and growing back as the next, unless your system asks for less motion; a colour with none is skipped, one colour alone stays still, and with none there is no count at all. A click anywhere on a card opens its ticket, so a card carries no button but Archive, an icon alone at the right end of that line, on a ticket that can be archived.

**Create** (⌘1; ⌘N too) sits first in the rail and opens a small picker: first each connected repository, to start planning there; under them the planning still open, with every plan drafted and not yet approved beside it and, under those, the specs your connected repositories hold that no planning and no ticket names, the way back into work whose plan is still to come. A planning is listed under its ticket's name, else its spec's title, and reads **Untitled** until the Architect or you title the spec — never the words cut from your first message to name its folder, and never in the spec itself, which has no title line meanwhile — taking its name the moment the spec or the plan gives it one ([D-118](11-open-decisions.md), [D-127](11-open-decisions.md)); the same name sits in the window's top bar just right of the sidebar toggle on each pane of that planning, and on no other page, since a ticket's contract, loop and stopped pages carry its name themselves. Opened from Home's empty state, the picker has nothing selected until an arrow key or the pointer picks a row. Once the plan is approved the ticket opens where its state is, and a stopped run on its stopped page ([D-130](11-open-decisions.md)). A planning you opened and put nothing into — no title, no spec, no message in the chat, no plan — is thrown away as you leave it, so clicking away from one you opened by mistake leaves nothing behind for the picker to offer; anything you have put into it stays, and closing Perbo is not leaving it: an untouched planning is still there when you come back ([D-129](11-open-decisions.md)). Its Spec pane holds the spec ([D-103](11-open-decisions.md)): a title and the five sections — Outcome, Requirements, No-Gos, Rabbit holes and Notes — each saved into the repository at `specs/<slug>/spec.md` when you leave it, with the folders created the first time. The slug comes from the title; a planning you have not titled takes one from your first message to the Architect, which the chat says as it names the folder, and the Architect is asked to give the spec a title of its own as it first writes it. A name you give there is kept: the plan is drafted under it and the ticket takes it, unless the Architect has titled the spec since. Otherwise the ticket takes the drafter's name and the spec takes it too. Each requirement is given an id when it is saved, and beside each id is the node its criteria sit in, or *none yet* before there is a plan — the requirements themselves are read in the section you write them in rather than listed again under it. The file is the spec: between one save and the next the repository holds it, so a spec you edited in your own editor is what the pane shows the next time it reads. Type `@` to name code and the pane offers the repository's exported names, with what each one is and the file it lives in; choosing one writes it in. The Architect writes through its own tools rather than this check, so what it does with a section you changed after it last read the file is the Architect's own behaviour. It writes the spec and stops there — it holds no tool that drafts a plan — so generating one is always your press, and there is one place to make it: **Generate plan**, which appears at the foot of this pane once the spec has a title and an outcome, under the spec it drafts from. Selecting a node opens it under the canvas in two equal halves: its criteria, each in a box you reword by hand with the ways it can be proven to pick from under it — the chat changes them too — which scroll on their own, and beside them one block that stays put, holding what it comes after and before side by side, then its paths, then, read-only, the Notes written on the page generated for it beside the spec — the one part of that page a person writes, the rest being what its card already shows. One contract has one approval, on the screen that says what it freezes, and on a plan already approved the same button reads **Open the contract** and goes straight there. **Start over from the spec…** asks first, as the Spec pane does. The pane also opens for a ticket already in `plan_review`: the picker lists it under Continue planning. Its **Impact pane** lists what this draft is likely to touch that its scope does not cover ([D-015](11-open-decisions.md)): files outside the scope that import what the draft changes or what the spec names, and paths in one of the classes Perbo recognises — a migration, a dependency manifest, configuration, something security-sensitive, CI, infrastructure or policy — inside a package the scope or the spec reaches. It is there once there is a plan — for a basic ticket, only while its check found something — and checks once on its own, when the planning first has a plan, because a plan is what impact is measured against; asking again after that is yours — **Check impact** before there is an answer and **Check again** after one, each a fresh index of the whole tracked tree — and the pane says what it was checked against and when. It is shown only where there is something to say. Words of your own on a question of one part are a sentence like any other and take the rest of the questions off, since the Architect answers what you said; the line in the conversation that says they were asked keeps them: hover or focus the **i** beside it and they are there. Each card the chat keeps for its own tools is one line — **Changed the plan**, or **Changing the plan** for a change that was refused — one that worked carrying the same **i**, so what a tool wrote is one hover away rather than filling the dock; a refused call adds the first sentence of why under its name, whole and wrapping over the lines it needs, with an **i** after it holding the whole reason where there is more, and a refusal shows its reason without asking, because that is the thing to act on. An edit behind that one has no card, because the graph already holds what it changed. Planning runs alongside a run: you can draft, save and compile a contract while a loop is going, and while one is going elsewhere. Starting a run, answering a decision, recording a finding, refreshing delivery, recording a product decision and checking a repository each take one at a time, and asking for a second says which one is in the way; disconnecting a repository, deleting a contract or changing its manifest waits for every command running in that repository ([D-101](11-open-decisions.md)).


### The contract editor and approval

The contract shows the outcome, the plan — an epic's execution graph, read-only, which pans and zooms and is changed only on the Graph pane, or a basic ticket's criteria, each with its expected evidence, which are edited right there while the plan waits for approval, each change written into the contract as it is made and the plan read against the spec as you confirm, and read-only once approved, the same rule on the contract tab and on the ticket's contract after approval ([D-NEW-basic-and-epic-flows](11-open-decisions.md)) — the allowed and prohibited paths, the base commit, the executor and reviewer models, each with its effort — each chosen here, because they are not among the four fields approving freezes and this is the last page before the loop starts; they read as stated text once the contract is approved — up to three engineering skills for the executor ([D-094](11-open-decisions.md)), and what stops this run — the stall window, the fact that nothing bounds its time, tokens or commands, and the ticket cost cap that applies only to an executor billed per token ([D-096](11-open-decisions.md)). Approving freezes four fields — outcome, criteria, scope, base — for good; how the work gets done stays the agent's. A checkbox asks whether to push the branch and open a pull request once the review gate passes; you still merge it yourself. Approving here is exactly `perbo approve`, run for you before the loop starts. It can be deleted here as a piece of work is from the picker, behind a confirmation that says what goes with it ([D-129](11-open-decisions.md)). For a plan in planning the contract is one of the planning's tabs, with Problems below it while a problem is open, and its other tabs are the way back into it; approving is refused while a problem is open, for an epic as for a basic ticket, whichever way you reached the contract, and an epic whose plan moved since the last reading is read on the Problems page first, as at **Confirm the plan** ([D-NEW-basic-and-epic-flows](11-open-decisions.md)); for a contract no planning curates, **Back to planning** goes to the ticket's own editor, because not being ready to approve means going back to change the plan; the spec is read and written in planning, and this page offers no second door to it.

### The run view and the review result

The loop screen names what's happening now against six stages — contract, execution, checks, decisions required, refinement, review — with a running description of steps taken, newest first, and a strip of commands used, cost spent and files touched beside the stall window that would stop it; no time estimate is invented. The page holds still and only the description of steps scrolls, inside its own box. From here you can open the worktree in your editor, stop the loop, or watch the agents' own output, the three buttons at the right end of their row: a transcript, a terminal pane, and the changed files — recorded output, not evidence, and the reviewer never reads the executor's narrative. The transcript is the executor's and the reviewer's own words, each marked as theirs and never a row for a tool call, in the order they were said with the latest at the bottom; while a run goes each turn appears as the run prints it, and the follow switch keeps the latest in view. Once the run has ended the same list is read back from the records, each attempt of the run in order: what its retained transcript holds of the executor's own session, never a subagent's, then each finding the reviewer filed that the attempt's review left open. A finding the runner states itself, such as a flaky check, prints as the runner's own line and is not listed as the reviewer's words. The run's log stands in until every attempt's transcript is read, and where no attempt retained the executor's words and the log holds them. **Export kept evidence** sits at the left of the page's bottom bar, and **Copy transcript** and **Back to the loop** at its right, the highlighted one rightmost. When a run ends short of a result — the review requested changes or escalated a decision to you, the reviewer's answer could not be parsed, refinement left findings open, the agent changed nothing, the guard refused a command, the attempt stalled or reached a limit the repository set — a card titled "The run ended", in the decision card's frame, says what ended it in one sentence read from the attempt the run recorded, in words and units rather than setting names, with an `i` holding the fuller reason from the same records: each command the guard refused and what it read it as, the review's open findings or its error as recorded, how long the agent was quiet, or the limit and what the attempt reached. Where the records hold no such verdict — the CLI refused to start, the run failed after the review approved, Perbo closed mid-command — the card says so and shows the command's own output, its error lines behind the `i`; another command that failed is titled with its own name. Once you confirm the card, its sentence and `i` head the description of steps, and the whole log stays with the agents' output. A run you stopped lands on a page of its own: at once from **Stop the loop**, without waiting for the stop to settle, and from Home, or from the Archive once it is filed, for as long as the ticket is stopped. The page holds the ticket's name, that the run was stopped, and the three things the records allow as buttons named and described nowhere, in one row at the bottom: **Delete this work** and **Plan it again** at its left, and **Open the contract**, **Watch what the agents did** and **Continue the task**, darkened, at its right end, the highlighted one rightmost, so nothing on it scrolls. **Continue the task** is another attempt against the same approved contract, carrying on from the last attempt's retained changes where it sealed any and starting the attempt again where it did not; it publishes as the stopped run was going to, by the contract page's checkbox, so nothing is asked again ([D-129](11-open-decisions.md)). **Plan it again** puts the work back before the loop: the approved contract is frozen ([ADR-0016](adr/0016-minimal-machine-maintained-planning.md)), so the stopped ticket is deleted with everything recorded after its contract — its attempts and the evidence they sealed — and leaves Home at the click, the spec it was drafted from stays, a new plan is admitted from it, and you land in planning on the new plan's Graph, or on its contract where the drafter did not divide it, with the work in **Create**'s picker as planning to continue; where the plan cannot be drafted once the ticket is deleted, you are told the ticket was deleted and that its spec is in **Create**'s picker to draft the plan from; it is offered for work drafted from a spec, and the spec has to still be there — a spec deleted since is said so, in the words the command itself refuses in. A run stopped outside the executor's window leaves the ticket stranded at the stage it reached, and a spec whose ticket is still live has its plan, so the button is disabled where the record is not spent, and says why only when you hover it; a ticket whose pull request is open is not offered it, because it is not deleted. **Delete this work** removes all of it, behind a confirmation — at every stage but one: a ticket whose pull request is open is refused, because that pull request is a record outside this machine, and it is closed or merged on GitHub first. A stop leaves the record where it stood — sealed at failed inside the executor's window, stranded at the stage it reached outside it — and none of these three changes that.

Once the gate closes, the review screen shows each criterion on a card led by its outcome — a tick where met, a cross where not met, a question mark where it cannot be determined — with how it was established as a badge: "directly verified" in green, "inferred" in amber where a proxy establishes it, and in red "evidence not retained" where it was only asserted and "not reviewed" where the review did not cover it; the table view says the same words in its Established column, and its evidence location behind an `i` beside it, then the deterministic checks and any open findings, with cost and elapsed time beside them; a review that ended on an error says why in the summary in one sentence — "The reviewer's answer could not be parsed." where no verdict the reviewer returned could be accepted, "review findings unavailable" for any other error — with the error as the review recorded it behind an `i` beside it ([D-NEW-nothing-shown-is-cut](11-open-decisions.md)); you can send a finding back for another look, export the evidence, or return to the contract to retry. Next always leads on to the merge screen, which asks one question — merge or don't — with the verified-criteria count, the checks, the scope and the base commit beside it; the desktop opens your browser to GitHub and never merges the pull request itself. Where the run kept its branch on your machine without publishing, the screen says so, and Merge on GitHub first pushes that branch and opens its pull request, with nothing run or reviewed again, then opens it the same way ([D-NEW-publish-a-retained-branch-later](11-open-decisions.md)); a refusal is said on the screen, and where there is no branch to publish, the screen says why. Either way the ticket returns you to Home with what changed.

### Completed tickets and the archive

A ticket stays on Home, marked complete, once it merges or closes — nothing files it away on its own. Archiving it, one at a time or all at once, moves it to the Archive: a searchable, filterable, sortable table with a diff column and CSV export, and a ticket filed by mistake returns to Home in one click. A stopped ticket deleted from its page, reached from Home or the Archive, leaves both lists at the click and does not come back while the delete finishes. A ticket whose run stopped is archived the same way, one at a time; one the loop still carries — running, or waiting on a decision, a merge or the queue — cannot be ([D-097](11-open-decisions.md)). The table fills the page with its count and pages under it, and shows no rows rather than a message when nothing matches.

### Settings, shortcuts and themes

The settings icon opens a pill of three sections. **General** holds your name, what can interrupt you and how (below), appearance — light, dark or system; text size; reduced motion — and AFK mode, which holds the machine awake for as long as a run or a decision is live. **Usage** answers whether you can start another ticket: each connected provider's own plan windows where it reports them, and this machine's ledger — spend this month, tickets run and tickets stopped short, average cost per merged ticket — read from retained attempt records, with unpriced attempts marked rather than guessed. **Connections** holds provider sign-in and refresh, the default model and effort per role, which a contract's choices start from, what stops a run on this machine — the stall window and the per-token ticket cost cap, which can tighten a repository's own limits and never loosen them — and repositories with their environment check and off-limits paths.

Every keyboard binding is rebindable per machine from Settings → General → Shortcuts: click one and press the new combination; a combination already in use is refused and named rather than silently taken. Approving a contract and opening a pull request stay fixed, since they are the two actions that spend money or write to your repository.

Decided, not built: a phone companion once pairing lands ([D-097](11-open-decisions.md)).

### When the loop interrupts you

The loop pauses for you only on a real decision — never for routine progress. One question at a time, with its evidence and, where the executor offered them, a few concrete options plus "let it decide" or your own words in a sentence. The card's buttons sit at its right, **Save and continue** rightmost, and Enter in the box you type in does what **Save and continue** does — Shift+Enter starts a new line, and Enter with nothing typed does nothing; every answer stays editable until you confirm the whole set, and confirming resumes the run. Outside a live decision, Perbo only notifies on four things — a decision is needed, a review finishes, a run stops short (it stalled, or hit a ceiling the repository set), or any stage changes — never on progress alone, and never twice for the same fact.

## The same work, from the command line

Everything above has a `perbo` command behind it, for scripting or a repository with no desktop open.

- **`perbo admit`** — one piece of work into a ticket and its contract, in `plan_review`. Type `--outcome`, `--criterion` and `--path` yourself, or let a model draft them from a GitHub issue (`--from owner/repo#412`) or a Markdown file (`--from-file issue.md`). Nothing runs until the draft is approved.
- **`perbo edit PRB-142`** — open the contract in `$VISUAL`/`$EDITOR`, or pass `--outcome`/`--criterion`/`--path` to change one part directly. Only while the ticket is in `plan_review`.
- **`perbo approve PRB-142`** — freezes the contract from that moment.
- **`perbo run --ticket PRB-142 [--publish]`** — provisions a worktree, executes, seals the change, runs the pinned checks, reviews independently, routes remediable findings back to the executor, and with `--publish` opens the pull request. A person still merges it.
- **`perbo inspect PRB-142 [--attempt <id>]`** — every attempt read back: how it ended, cost, checks, the review and where each finding went; `--verify` recomputes an attempt's stored hashes instead of reading it.
- **`perbo list [--all]`** — the admitted work and where each ticket stands.
- **`perbo sync PRB-142 [--merge]`** — reads the pull request, its checks and its stop answers from GitHub onto the ticket; `--merge` additionally merges it, but only where the repository has opted into `merge: loop` and a separate review run has approved that exact head ([D-041](11-open-decisions.md)).
- **`perbo stops`** — precision of stopping, read live from the answers on pull requests: of the changes with an answer, the share a person endorsed, beside the share of changes a person was shown anything on, and beside it the share of merges that went out unattended.
- **`perbo escapes`** — of the tickets that merged: how many were reverted, and how many separately had a touched path changed again within fourteen days.
- **`perbo serve`** — the queue over the ticket store. Each tick it fetches the base, syncs and, where configured, merges open pull requests in dependency and priority order, starts ready tickets up to the configured concurrency, and drafts tickets from a tracker label if one is configured. While it runs it hosts a local endpoint for your own session.
- **`perbo agent [--provider claude|codex]`** — your own Claude Code or Codex session in the checkout, with that endpoint injected. That session can read every ticket and its evidence, admit new tickets as drafts, edit an unapproved contract, sync a pull request and pause the queue — never approve, publish or merge; that stays a person's keystroke. Needs a running `serve`.
- **`perbo interview --spec specs/<slug>`** — your own session, Claude Code through the Claude Agent SDK or Codex through `codex app-server`, questioning you about a piece of work and writing the spec. It reads anything and runs read-only commands; the spec folder, `CONTEXT.md` and the ADR folder are the only places it writes, and anything else is refused rather than put to you — on Codex the app server's own approval questions are answered by the same rules rather than reaching you. It writes the spec, and once you have drafted the plan from it, changes that plan only through the validated edit path, recorded as the Architect's; it cannot approve, publish or merge ([D-102](11-open-decisions.md)). The desktop starts it on Claude Opus 5.5 wherever Claude Code's catalog offers that model, and otherwise on the planning's executor model, which is the model its spec is drafted with.
- **`perbo principle add "..."`** — records a product answer no established practice could settle; every later executor brief consults it. This is what the desktop's decision screen writes on your behalf when you answer one.
- **`perbo verdict PRB-142 --decide <finding key> --choice approach|let-it-decide|ship-as-is --note "..."`** — records your answer on the finding the review routed to you: your approach or the executor's goes to one remediation round, and shipping it as it is settles it. The desktop's decision screen writes one per question as well as the principle ([D-132](11-open-decisions.md)).

## What you see, and what the loop handles

The loop is the product; independent review is one step inside it, not the thing you interact with directly ([D-088](11-open-decisions.md)). Admitted work runs end to end — execution, checks, review, repair — and a person sees only what needs a decision ([D-001](11-open-decisions.md)). Every finding the executor can close against an established practice, it closes without you; only a `security.*` or `context.*` finding, a deterministic check that failed, the final remediation round, or a point the executor declares it has no practice for reaches you, in the desktop's decision screen or a pull request's checkboxes ([D-065](11-open-decisions.md)). The decision screen gives each finding the review routed to you three answers, where the review judged the whole change ([D-132](11-open-decisions.md)); any other question, one on a review that could not judge every criterion or did not complete, or one whose closer the reviewer named as you, takes your words alone, recorded as a principle for the executor. You can type your approach, which is the box the screen opens on. **Let it decide** leaves the approach to the executor within the approved contract. **Ship as it is** is a deliberate third choice, never selected for you, and delivers the change unchanged for that finding. An approach, or a decision left to the executor, sends the finding back for one round with your words, and the round is checked closed rather than reviewed again. A finding shipped as it is is settled by your answer. Once nothing you were asked is open, the run goes on to delivery, publishing as the run that stopped for you was going to where you answered a finding, and the pull request lists what you decided in your own words; words alone, with no finding answered, publish nothing. A person picks the model and provider for Perbo's own review ([D-088](11-open-decisions.md)). Decided, not built: reviews other tools leave on the same pull request are read and routed like Perbo's own findings.
